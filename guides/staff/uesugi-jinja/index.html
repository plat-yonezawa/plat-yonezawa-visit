Interpreter's Note: Answering "When Was It Built?"

If asked when the shrine was founded, the accurate answer comes in two steps: shrine rites began in 1871, and the shrine buildings went up at the current site in 1876. That's why some sources say 1871 and others 1876 (Kazawa Masato's doctoral dissertation sorts this out).

The story that he was placed in a jar still wearing armor comes from an Edo-period record, the Kenshin Nenpu. Please add "according to tradition."

Kenshin's remains are not under the current shrine. In 1876 they were moved to the center of the Mausoleum, and the monument there is carved not with "Kenshin" but with his real name, "Terutora." A curator at the Keishōden has also said there is "no way to confirm it," so avoid stating it as fact; it's safest to add "that is what the records say."

The statue of Bishamonten once enshrined in the memorial hall is now at nearby Hōon-ji Temple (a Yamagata Prefecture Designated Cultural Property).

To enjoy Uesugi Shrine, it helps to remember four names. They make the story much easier to follow.

  • Uesugi Kenshin (1530–1578): A Sengoku-period daimyō of Echigo (Niigata), famous for the Battles of Kawanakajima, which he fought again and again against Takeda Shingen. He was deeply devoted to BishamontenA Buddhist guardian deity, also worshipped as a god of war. Kenshin was especially devoted to him and, according to tradition, flew the character "毘" (Bi) on his banners., the Buddhist guardian god of war
  • Uesugi Kagekatsu (1556–1623): Kenshin's adopted son and heir. He moved to Aizu and then to Yonezawa, becoming the first lord of the Yonezawa Domain
  • Naoe Kanetsugu (1560–1620): The chief retainer who supported Kagekatsu. Known for the character "愛" (love) on his helmet, he laid the foundations of the town of Yonezawa
  • Uesugi Yōzan (1751–1822): The lord who, in the mid-Edo period, turned around the debt-ridden Yonezawa Domain. He is known for the saying "Naseba naru" ("If you try, it can be done")

From 1.2 Million Koku to 300,000 Koku

After opposing Tokugawa Ieyasu at the Battle of Sekigahara (1600), the Uesugi family saw its lands cut to a quarter: from 1.2 million koku (a measure of a domain's rice yield) in Aizu to 300,000 koku in Yonezawa. Even so, they let go of almost none of their retainers, so the Yonezawa Domain struggled with its finances for a long time. The lord who turned the domain around was Yōzan. Yōzan passed on his principles for a lord to his successor. These are the Denkoku no JiThree precepts that Uesugi Yōzan gave to the next lord as guidance. They express the idea that "the domain and its people do not belong to the lord.", whose monument stands at Matsugasaki Shrine.

Legend and Fact

Many famous stories are told about Kenshin. But not all of them can be confirmed in records from his time.

  • The Sixteen House Precepts: Widely known as Kenshin's teachings, but thought to have been compiled later, in the Edo period
  • Sending salt to an enemy: The famous tale of Kenshin sending salt to Takeda Shingen cannot be confirmed in records from the time
  • The duel at Kawanakajima: The scene of Kenshin and Shingen crossing swords one-on-one spread later, through stories and folding screens
✨ Why the Legends Still Matter
So many legends grew up because Kenshin was so deeply loved. Uesugi Shrine itself stands as proof of "the history of people who have cherished Kenshin as a god."
At the same time, some things can be said for certain from the records: many warlords relied on Kenshin; he prayed earnestly to the gods and Buddhas before battle; and he loved sake and had beautiful handwriting.

  • 送鹽給敵人：送鹽給武田信玄的美談，無法在當時的紀錄中獲得證實

✨ 即便如此，傳說仍然重要的理由
之所以誕生這麼多傳說，正是因為謙信深受人們敬愛。上杉神社本身，也是「人們將謙信奉為神明、珍視至今的歷史」的見證。

Interpreter's Note: Handling the Legends

A curator at the Keishōden says there is no support in sources from Kenshin's time for the Sixteen Precepts, the salt story, or the phrase "the warlord of righteousness (gi)." Well-informed visitors sometimes point out, "That's not true."

On the other hand, the Sixteen Precepts appear on the shrine's amulet bags and elsewhere. Rather than denying them, it's more tactful to say, "This has been handed down as a story that arose in later times."

"Gi" (義) is often translated into English as righteousness, but please avoid stating flatly that "Kenshin fought for righteousness."

通訳者のための想定Q&A

何の神さまですか？
戦国武将の上杉謙信です。授与品には「勝ち守」などがあります。「勝利の神さま」と言い切るのは避けてください。
謙信のお墓はここですか？
いいえ。遺骸は1876年に、少し離れた上杉家の御廟所へ移されました。
いつできた神社ですか？
1871年に神社としてのお祭りが始まり、1876年にいまの場所に社殿ができました。いまの社殿は1923年の再建です。
国宝はありますか？
稽照殿に国宝はありません。重要文化財は16件です。国宝の洛中洛外図屏風は、隣の上杉博物館にあります。
「愛」の兜は見られますか？
展示の時期が限られます。最新の展示は稽照殿に問い合わせるよう案内してください。
稽照殿の中で写真を撮れますか？
撮れません。
なぜ新潟の武将が山形にまつられているの？
跡を継いだ景勝が会津、そして米沢へ移るとき、謙信の遺骸を納めたお堂もいっしょに移したからです。
「敵に塩を送った」のは本当？
広く知られた話ですが、当時の記録では確かめられていません。「そう伝わっています」と答えてください。
この建物は誰が設計したの？
米沢出身の建築家・伊東忠太です。明治神宮や築地本願寺も手がけました。
カードは使えますか？
使えません。授与所も稽照殿も現金のみです。

Expected Q&A for Interpreters

What deity is enshrined here?
The Sengoku-period warlord Uesugi Kenshin. The amulets include a "Kachi-mamori" (victory amulet), among others. Avoid flatly calling him "the god of victory."
Is Kenshin's grave here?
No. His remains were moved in 1876 to the Uesugi Family Mausoleum, a short distance away.
When was the shrine founded?
Shrine rites began in 1871, and the shrine buildings went up at the current site in 1876. The current buildings are a 1923 reconstruction.
Are there any National Treasures?
The Keishōden has no National Treasures. It has 16 Important Cultural Properties. The National Treasure Rakuchū Rakugai-zu folding screens are at the Uesugi Museum next door.
Can I see the "愛" (love) helmet?
It is only on display at certain times. Please suggest checking with the Keishōden for the latest exhibits.
Can I take photos inside the Keishōden?
No, you can't.
Why is a warlord from Niigata enshrined in Yamagata?
Because when his successor Kagekatsu moved to Aizu and then to Yonezawa, he brought along the hall holding Kenshin's remains.
Is it true that he "sent salt to his enemy"?
It's a well-known story, but it can't be confirmed in records from the time. Please answer, "That's how the story goes."
Who designed this building?
Itō Chūta, an architect from Yonezawa. He also designed Meiji Jingū and Tsukiji Hongan-ji.
Can I pay by card?
No. Both the amulet office and the Keishōden are cash only.

給口譯員的模擬問答

這裡供奉的是什麼神？
是戰國武將上杉謙信。授與品中有「勝守」等。請避免斷言是「勝利之神」。
謙信的墓在這裡嗎？
不是。遺骸已於1876年移至稍遠處的上杉家御廟所。
這座神社是什麼時候建的？
1871年開始以神社形式舉行祭祀，1876年在現址建成社殿。現在的社殿是1923年重建的。
有國寶嗎？
稽照殿沒有國寶，重要文化財有16件。國寶洛中洛外圖屏風收藏在隔壁的上杉博物館。
看得到「愛」字頭盔嗎？
展出期間有限。請引導對方向稽照殿洽詢最新的展出資訊。
稽照殿內可以拍照嗎？
不行。
為什麼新潟的武將會供奉在山形？
因為繼承家業的景勝移往會津、再移往米澤時，將安放謙信遺骸的佛堂也一同遷移。
「送鹽給敵人」是真的嗎？
這是廣為人知的故事，但無法在當時的紀錄中獲得證實。請回答「據傳是這樣」。
這棟建築是誰設計的？
米澤出身的建築師伊東忠太。他也設計了明治神宮與築地本願寺。
可以刷卡嗎？
不行。授與所與稽照殿都只收現金。

新潟で亡くなった謙信は、約300年かけて米沢にたどり着き、町の人びとの手で神さまとしてまつられてきました。大火のあとも、人びとは社を建て直しました。帰り道、もう一度鳥居をふり返ってみてください。

Kenshin, who died in Niigata, reached Yonezawa after a journey of some 300 years, and the people of the town have honored him as a god ever since. Even after the great fire, they rebuilt his shrine. On your way out, take one more look back at the torii.

在新潟辭世的謙信，歷經約300年才來到米澤，由當地人們親手奉為神明供奉至今。大火之後，人們也重建了神社。回程時，請再回頭看一眼鳥居。
